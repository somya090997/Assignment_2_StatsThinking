Data Cleaning

Before any analysis was conducted, missing-data placeholders were identified and replaced with NA rather than removed. Per the variable dictionary, budget values of exactly $0 or below $10,000 are placeholder entries, and revenue values of $0 indicate missing box-office data. Rather than dropping these rows entirely, the affected values were converted to NA, retaining all 3,426 films in the five focus genres. Rows with NA budget or revenue are excluded automatically by R when calculating financial summaries and plots, but are retained for analyses such as franchise composition that do not require financial data. ### Summary tables

The typical film cost $25M to produce (median budget) and returned 2.3x its budget at the box office (median ROI). The mean ROI of 13.6x is not a reliable benchmark — it is driven almost entirely by a handful of micro-budget Horror films with extreme returns, most notably Paranormal Activity (~12,890x ROI). Franchise films represent just 7% of the historical slate, with Originals making up the remaining 93%.

Visualisations

Both budget and ROI are right-skewed, so a log10 scale is applied to the relevant axes to make distributions readable. The break-even line (ROI = 1) is marked on all ROI plots as a reference.

trend_summary <- movies_clean |>
  filter(release_year >= 2000, release_year <= 2016) |>
  group_by(release_year) |>
  summarise(
    n_films       = n(),
    median_budget = median(budget_m,  na.rm = TRUE),
    median_roi    = median(roi,        na.rm = TRUE),
    pct_franchise = sum(franchise_status == "Franchise") / n() * 100,
    .groups       = "drop"
  )

Franchise films make up 7% of the overall slate. Horror (21%) and Action (11%) have the highest franchise concentration; Drama sits lowest at just 1%.

Three trend lines

trend_summary |>
  pivot_longer(
    cols      = c(median_budget, median_roi, pct_franchise),
    names_to  = "metric",
    values_to = "value"
  ) |>
  mutate(metric = recode(metric,
    median_budget  = "Median Budget (USD millions)",
    median_roi     = "Median ROI",
    pct_franchise  = "Franchise Share (%)"
  )) |>
  ggplot(aes(x = release_year, y = value)) +
  geom_line(colour = "#5B8DB8", linewidth = 0.9) +
  geom_point(colour = "#5B8DB8", size = 1.8) +
  geom_smooth(method = "loess", se = FALSE,
              colour = "#C9485B", linewidth = 0.9, linetype = "dashed") +
  facet_wrap(~ metric, ncol = 1, scales = "free_y") +
  labs(
    title    = "Slate Trends Over Time (2000–2016)",
    subtitle = "Red dashed = LOESS trend",
    x        = "Release Year",
    y        = NULL
  ) +
  scale_x_continuous(breaks = seq(2000, 2016, 4))
